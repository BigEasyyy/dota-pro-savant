                Each number is how much one standard deviation of that stat moves a player's odds of winning. Zero means the stat added nothing once the others were known. Each stat may only count in its sensible direction, so a support can't earn credit by dying more while their cores do well.

                How these numbers are worked out

                The data holds every OpenDota-tracked game in leagues rated premium or professional, from the first day of The International 2025 to the latest refresh. OpenDota also marks some non-events as professional, so — leagues are dropped (open ladders and club leagues running longer than 150 days, in-house leagues with fewer than 4 teams or mostly unregistered teams, and streamer shows), along with single games where either side isn’t a registered team. That removes — games. Every stat, position and percentile on the page is recomputed from single matches for the dates and opponents you pick.

                Opponents. A team counts as Tier 1 for a game if it played 5+ games at Tier 1 main events (TI, DreamLeague, PGL Wallachia, ESL One, BLAST SLAM, FISSURE PLAYGROUND, Esports World Cup; never qualifiers) within six months of that game. — teams are Tier 1 right now. Tier 2 adds registered teams rated 1500+ Elo going into the game; All adds everyone else. The default shows only games against Tier 1 teams.

                Positions. A player gets a separate stat line for every position where they reach the minimum games, using only those games, so percentiles always compare like with like. Their main position is the qualifying one they played most. Players who split positions get a switch on their page.

                Position comes from the lane first. OpenDota records which lane each player spent the early game in. On each team, a lone mid laner is Pos 2; in the safe lane the richer of the two players is Pos 1 and the other Pos 5; in the off lane the richer is Pos 3 and the other Pos 4. That covers —% of pro games. The rest (unparsed games, trilanes, jungle and roaming setups) fall back to the hero, then gold: — heroes are played in one position —%+ of the time and claim it; everyone else fills the open positions by gold per minute, richest first. The other — heroes are multi-position.

                xWin % works like xwOBA. One model per position predicts whether a player's team wins from that player's own match: their gold, XP, last-hit and deny lead over the opposing player in the same position at 10:00, plus whole-game contribution measured as a share of their own team (gold, hero damage, tower damage, healing, kill participation, deaths) and stuns, wards and stacks per minute. Shares of the player's own team are used instead of raw totals because the winning team farms more, so raw totals would mostly measure who won. xWin % is the average prediction over the selected dates. Two adjustments come first. The 10:00 lane numbers are measured against what that hero usually does in that position, so a roaming support or a hero that always gives up lane isn’t marked down for it. Then every stat is adjusted for the opponent’s Elo going into the game, so the same lead counts for more against a stronger team. The ± beside xWin % on a player page is its standard error: with fewer games, the true value could be further off.

                Replay stats. For nearly every pro game OpenDota also reads the full replay, which adds: lane results (each lane’s combined net worth at 10:00 against the lane it faced; 10% ahead is a win, 10% behind a loss, anything closer a draw), the time of each player’s first 2,000+ gold item and Black King Bar, compared with the usual time for that hero in that position, time spent dead, enemy wards destroyed, how long their observer wards survived (a ward gone before 340 of its 360 seconds counts as destroyed), runes picked up, towers and Roshans last-hit, and first blood. A bar shows “not enough data” when a player has too little of something to rank, such as a carry who never places wards. Deciding games are the last possible game of a series (game 3 of a best-of-3, game 5 of a best-of-5). Consistency is how much a player’s xWin moves from game to game.

                Closing and late games. Leads closed is the share of games a player’s team won after being 5,000+ gold ahead at 20:00 (about 92% across all pro games); comebacks are wins from 5,000+ behind (about 9%). These lean on the whole team, so they are most telling on team pages. Late-game xWin is xWin in games of 45 minutes or more; scaling is that minus xWin in games under 30 minutes. Bars drawn faded and striped rest on a small sample: under 30 games overall, or too few of the games that stat needs (for example fewer than 12 long games or 15 games with a big lead).

                Small samples. A player’s xWin % and lane xWin % are pulled toward their position’s average, as if they had also played a set number of perfectly average games: about 20–40, measured each week from how much one player’s games vary compared with how much players truly differ. With 20 games, a raw 66% might show as about 60%; with 100 games it barely moves. Player pages show the raw figure under the big number. WAA uses the raw figures, since a short sample already earns less of it.

                Lane xWin % uses only the 10:00 numbers, so it rates the early game. Wins Above Average (WAA) adds up, over every game, the player's xWin minus the average xWin for their position in the selected dates. A typical player at the position scores 0. Like WAR, it counts: playing more games well adds up. The model separates good and bad support play less sharply than core play, so WAA spreads wider for cores. Compare WAA within a position.

                On games it never saw (— onward) the model's AUC is —, where 0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 is perfect. Lane-only AUC is —. Splitting each player's games into alternating halves, xWin % in one half matches the other at r = —, against r = — for actual win %, so xWin tracks the player more than their team's results do.

                Percentiles rank a player against everyone with the same position in the selected dates, from 1 (worst) to 100 (best). Deaths are reversed, so fewer deaths gives a higher percentile. Each position shows the stats that matter most for it. Fewer than 30 games is marked as a small sample.

                Refresh history. The data and model are rebuilt every Monday. Each run is logged here, and its model weights are archived alongside the refresh script.
